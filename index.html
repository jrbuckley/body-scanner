    How it works: line yourself up until every check turns green, hold still — the countdown starts on its own. You turn the same direction in 45° steps: front, 45°, side (90°), 135°, then back (180°) — the two diagonal shots and the back shot are what let the 3D model measure the corners of your body instead of guessing them as an ellipse. The two diagonals are different stances: 45° is a front three-quarter (your face still toward the camera); 135° is a back three-quarter (your face turned away). The checks tell them apart by your shoulders: past 90° their left-right order in the picture flips compared with the front shot. Voice guidance (the Voice button above, on by default) calls each position out loud as its step comes up, says “hold still” when you're lined up, and reads a correction out loud only if it has stayed on screen for a few seconds — the messages flicker too fast while you move to speak each one. The moment a shot is captured, the pose model runs on the still right here and the numbers appear under Analysis — deltas compare against your station baseline once it's been set from your first station session. Downloads land as progress-YYYY-MM-DD-<view>.jpg if you want the files. Front + side alone still upload fine if you stop early — the extra views just add points. Uploading sends the photos too: a web-size copy of each captured view goes to the backend with its data and lands in The Golden Path on its next scanner sync, so the session's pictures sit next to the 3D model without a separate upload step. If a photo fails (weak signal), the data still lands and the next Upload tap retries just the missing photos.

    Protocol that keeps the ratios honest: same spot and distance, turn in place (don't step sideways), arms relaxed at your sides, similar shorts, camera at about waist height.
